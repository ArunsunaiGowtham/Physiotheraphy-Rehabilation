5 Proven Exercises for Sciatica and Lower Back Pain Relief

Dr. Sarah Jenkins, PT, DPT, OCS Published: September 2, 2026 6 min read

Sciatica is not a distinct medical disease in itself, but rather a clinical symptom of irritation or mechanical compression affecting the large sciatic nerve roots that exit your lower lumbar spine (L4 to S3).

When a lumbar disc bulges posteriorly or the piriformis muscle spasms chronically, intense shooting pain, electric tingling, or numbness can travel from your glute down the back of your leg past the knee. Thankfully, conservative physical therapy movements can create space in the spinal canal and relieve nerve root irritation without surgical intervention.

"Centralization is the gold standard goal: as you perform the correct McKenzie extensions, you should notice your radiating leg pain retreating upward toward your lower back. This confirms nerve decompression."

— Dr. Sarah Jenkins, Clinical Director

1. The Prone Press-Up (McKenzie Extension)

Lie flat on your stomach with your hands positioned underneath your shoulders as if about to do a pushup. Keeping your hips and pelvic bones relaxed on the floor, gently press your upper torso upward. Exhale at the top and hold for 2 to 3 seconds before lowering down.

Dosage: 10 repetitions, performed 3 to 4 times throughout the day.

2. Sciatic Nerve Flossing (Neural Mobilization)

Sit tall on the edge of a sturdy chair. Slump your shoulders gently and tuck your chin toward your chest. As you extend your affected leg straight out in front of you, point your toes downward toward the floor. As you flex your foot back toward your shin, simultaneously tilt your head back up. This dynamic glide frees the nerve from fascial adhesions without over-stretching it.

Dosage: 15 gentle, rhythmic repetitions. Never push into sharp pain.

3. Figure-4 Piriformis Stretch

Lie on your back with both knees bent and feet flat on the floor. Cross the ankle of your painful leg over the opposite knee to form a figure-4 shape. Reach your hands behind the uninjured thigh and pull it gently toward your chest until a deep, comfortable stretch is felt in the outer glute.

Dosage: Hold for 30 seconds, repeat 3 times per side.

Anatomical Landmark: Lumbar Nerve Roots

Notice how nerve roots exit between the L4, L5, and S1 vertebrae. Compression here is what triggers classic sciatica symptoms.
